# Anthropic, unofficial study

## Direction

Reference: the captured anthropic.com home (2026-09-21, 1440x900, 3,539px). Mood: a research journal printed on warm paper, with one open sky. Signature: three type roles held strictly apart - sans for statements, serif for prose, mono for metadata - and a sky whose peach clouds drift almost imperceptibly.

## System

- Type: Inter 500 for headlines and UI, Source Serif 4 for prose and the sky headline, JetBrains Mono uppercase for card metadata labels.
- Palette: cream `#f0eee6`, tan cards `#e3dacc`, ink `#141413`, grey `#5e5d59`, hairline `#d1cdc2`; sky `#7ea5e0 -> #b7cdef` with peach cloud bands.
- Motion density: split headline, blur-in sky title, slow cloud drift (paused offscreen), card lift, topic expand.

## Techniques

- [Gradient atmosphere](../../expression/techniques/gradient-atmosphere.md) for the painted sky.
- Split button with a divider and chevron cell in the header.

## Layout Floor

Sticky cream header that gains a hairline on scroll; intro, cards, and mission stack below 860-900px.

## Comparison With The Original

Reviewed side by side at 1440x900 against 4 capture frames (`.tmp/compare/anthropic.jpg`).

- Matches: a cream header with an uppercase name, small nav links, and a dark split button with a chevron cell; a bold three-line sans statement with underlined words beside a serif paragraph; a wide rounded sky panel with peach clouds, a staggered two-line serif headline, a serif sub, and a small white button; a black band; "Latest releases" with three tan cards (sans title, serif body, mono DATE/CATEGORY/DETAILS rows with underlined detail links, dark button); a bold mission line beside a hairline topic list with grey categories; a dark footer link grid.
- Deliberate differences: the sky is painted gradients with drifting blurred clouds rather than a photograph; every release, date, and line of copy is fictional; the logo mark is not drawn; topic rows expand in place.
- Remaining gaps: study 2,415px vs 3,539px; the original's footer lists many more link columns; the sky's clouds are softer and less textured than the original's photographic clouds.
